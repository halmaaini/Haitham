01 · The Movement

A column wheel, not a cam.

The chronograph is governed by a column wheel cut from a single blank of steel. Start, stop, return — each command passes through the same seven teeth. The gear train runs on nineteen jewels. The balance beats at 21,600 an hour.

Every bridge is bevelled by hand and drawn with Geneva stripes. You will see this work through the hunter case back, and nowhere else.

02 · The Dial

Silver, grained, printed once.

The dial is solid silver, grained by hand with a boxwood brush. Numerals are printed in a single pass of black enamel — no second strike, no correction. The hands are steel, heated over a flame until they turn the blue of deep water.

The crystal above them is sapphire, domed, and colder to the touch than the case that holds it.

03 · Enquiries

Twelve pieces a year.

Each Almaini is assembled by one watchmaker, start to finish, and signed under the balance cock. Delivery runs eleven months from commission. We answer every letter.
